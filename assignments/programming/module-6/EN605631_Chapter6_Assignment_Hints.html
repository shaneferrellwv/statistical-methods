EN.605.631 — Chapter 6 Assignment Hints (Joint Distributions)

Use these hints to plan your derivations and sanity‑check results. Include small tables, sketches of regions, or brief simulations to validate edge cases.

Quick roadmap

  • State the model: joint pmf/pdf p_{X,Y}(x,y) or f_{X,Y}(x,y) and the support region S (grid or subset of ℝ²).
  • Marginals: p_X(x)=∑_y p_{X,Y}(x,y) or f_X(x)=∫ f_{X,Y}(x,y) dy (integrate/sum over the other variable’s support).
  • Conditionals: p_{X|Y}(x|y) = p_{X,Y}(x,y)/p_Y(y); f_{X|Y}(x|y) = f_{X,Y}(x,y)/f_Y(y) where denominators > 0.
  • Independence: check factorization (p_{X,Y}=p_X p_Y) on the support.
  • Expectations: bivariate LOTUS E[g(X,Y)] = ∑∑ g(x,y)p_{X,Y}(x,y) or ∬ g(x,y) f_{X,Y}(x,y) dxdy.
  • Covariance/Correlation: Cov(X,Y)=E[XY]-E[X]E[Y]; note Cov=0 does not imply independence in general.
  • Sums: use convolution (discrete sum/integral) for X+Y, or recognize named family closures (Normal, Gamma/chi‑square, Poisson).
  • Transformations: for U=g(X,Y), V=h(X,Y), map the region and use Jacobian |det(∂(x,y)/∂(u,v))|. Prefer the cdf method if non‑invertible.

Problem 1 — Joint CDF/pmf basics & marginals (10 pts)

  • Draw the support: for discrete, mark the lattice points; for continuous, shade the region in ℝ².
  • From F_{X,Y}(x,y), recover the joint mass/density by differences/partials; from pmf/pdf, verify F increases to 1.
  • Compute marginals by summing/integrating over the other variable’s domain; confirm they each sum/integrate to 1.
  • Check edge cases: F(∞,∞)=1, F(−∞,y)=F(x,−∞)=0; monotone & right‑continuous in each argument.

Problem 2 — Independence diagnostics (discrete & continuous) (12 pts)

  • Test independence by factorization: p_{X,Y}(x,y) ?= p_X(x)p_Y(y) (or f_{X,Y}=f_X f_Y).
  • For discrete counterexamples, use a small 2×2 table with margins 0.5/0.5 each but a non‑factorable joint.
  • For continuous, integrate to get marginals first; watch for support‑restricted dependence (e.g., triangular region).
  • If Cov(X,Y)=0, still check factorization—uncorrelated ≠ independent (unless jointly normal).

Problem 3 — Convolution & comparison (12 pts)

  • If Z=X+Y: discrete p_Z(z)=∑_x p_X(x)p_Y(z−x); continuous f_Z(z)=∫ f_X(x)f_Y(z−x) dx.
  • Sketch the line x+y=z slicing through the support to set integration/summation limits correctly.
  • Compare to a known family when possible (e.g., sum of iid Poissons is Poisson; normals add to normal).
  • Sanity checks: E[Z]=E[X]+E[Y]; Var(Z)=Var(X)+Var(Y) if independence holds.

Problem 4 — Conditional distributions (discrete & continuous) (12 pts)

  • Compute p_{X|Y}(x|y) / f_{X|Y}(x|y) as joint over marginal; confirm it integrates/sums to 1 in x.
  • For continuous triangular/rectangular supports, draw cross‑sections at fixed y to see the conditional support.
  • Use conditional expectation E[X|Y=y] to compute (law of total expectation).
  • Similarly for variance: Var(X) = E[Var(X|Y)] + Var(E[X|Y]) (law of total variance).

Problem 5 — Order statistics & the range (12 pts)

  • For iid continuous X_1,…,X_n with cdf F and pdf f, order statistics:
    • f_{X_{(k)}}(x) = {n\choose k-1} [F(x)]^{k-1} [1-F(x)]^{n-k} f(x).
    • f_{X_{(1)},X_{(n)}}(u,v) has support u≤v; start from joint density of all order stats or use gap transforms.
  • The range R=X_{(n)}-X_{(1)}: derive via joint of min/max then transform to (U,V)=(min, range); check Jacobian = 1.
  • For Uniform(0,1), results simplify greatly; use as a check or as the core of your derivation.

Problem 6 — Sums of independent gammas & chi-square (10 pts)

  • If X_i ~ Gamma(α_i, λ) independent (common rate λ), then ∑ X_i ~ Gamma(α_•, λ) with α_•=∑ α_i.
  • Chi‑square with ν df is Gamma(α=ν/2, λ=1/2); sums of independent chi‑squares add df.
  • Use mgfs to justify closure quickly: M_{Gamma}(t) = (λ/(λ−t))^{α} for t<λ.
  • Translate between rate/ scale conventions carefully; keep one consistent parameterization.

Problem 7 — Transformations & Jacobians (bivariate) (16 pts)

  • For a bijective transform (U,V)=T(X,Y), compute the inverse (X,Y)=T^{-1}(U,V) and the Jacobian |det(∂(x,y)/∂(u,v))|.
  • Map the region: transform boundaries carefully; draw a few level curves to avoid algebraic mistakes.
  • If the map is many‑to‑one (e.g., (R,Θ) from (X,Y) for polar), sum contributions from all preimages.
  • Prefer the cdf method for non‑invertible g(X,Y); differentiate at the end for densities.

Problem 8 — Sums & mixtures in practice (Poisson splitting / multinomial) (16 pts)

  • Poisson splitting: if N ~ Pois(λ) and each event independently classified into categories with probs p_i, then counts are independent Pois(λ p_i) and jointly multinomial given N.
  • Use the law of total probability: unconditional joint pmf is a Poisson–multinomial mixture; sum over N.
  • Validate by checking sums to 1 and matching marginal Pois(λ p_i) distributions.
  • For sums/mixtures in practice, simulate small examples to verify independence and marginals.

Common pitfalls

  • Forgetting to describe the support region precisely before integrating/summing.
  • Using independence without verifying factorization on the support.
  • Mis‑setting convolution limits (draw x+y=z lines).
  • Dropping the absolute value in the Jacobian or missing multiple preimages.
  • Treating Cov=0 as independence (only true for special families, e.g., jointly normal).
  • Not checking that conditionals integrate/sum to 1 for each conditioning value.
