EN.605.631 — Chapter 7 Assignment Hints (Expectation, Covariance, MGFs)

Use these hints to plan your derivations before you compute. Pair each result with a quick check: limiting cases, symmetry, or a tiny simulation.

Quick roadmap

  • Linearity first: E[∑ X_i] = ∑ E[X_i] (no independence needed). Indicators: E[1_A] = P(A).
  • Covariance/correlation: Cov(X,Y)=E[XY]-E[X]E[Y]; Var(∑ X_i)=∑ Var(X_i) + 2∑_{i<j} Cov(X_i,X_j). Independence ⇒ Cov=0 but not conversely.
  • Conditional tools: E[X]=E[E[X|Y]] (law of total expectation); Var(X)=E[Var(X|Y)] + Var(E[X|Y]).
  • MGFs: M_X(t)=E[e^{tX}]; M_{aX+b}(t)=e^{bt} M_X(at); for independent sums, M_{X+Y}=M_X M_Y. Identify distributions via matching mgfs and get moments by differentiating at 0.
  • Random walks & runs: represent with sums of i.i.d. increments or indicator counts; use linearity/variance additivity and (when applicable) independence.

Problem 1 — Linearity of Expectation & Indicators (10 pts)

  • Translate counts to indicator variables: if X counts occurrences of events A_i, write X=∑ I_i with I_i=1_{A_i}.
  • Then E[X]=∑ P(A_i) regardless of dependence. For pair counts, use E[X]=∑_{i<j} P(A_i∩A_j).
  • When events are symmetric, compute one probability and multiply. Use complements when direct probabilities are messy.

Problem 2 — Expectation of Common Distributions (12 pts)

  • Recall quick facts you can cite without re‑derivation:
    • Bernoulli/Indicator: E[p] = p, Var = p(1-p); Binomial: E[np], Var=np(1-p); Poisson: E=Var=λ.
    • Geometric (trials to first success): E=1/p, Var=(1-p)/p^2; NegBin (r‑th success): E=r/p, Var=r(1-p)/p^2.
    • Hypergeometric: E = n K/N, Var = n (K/N)(1-K/N) ((N-n)/(N-1)).
    • Exponential: E=1/λ; Gamma(shape α, rate λ): E=α/λ; Normal: E=μ.
  • If the problem requests a derivation, use LOTUS or MGF differentiation: E[X]=M_X'(0), E[X^2]=M_X''(0).

Problem 3 — Classical Expectation Problems (12 pts)

  • For classical puzzles (e.g., matching, occupancy, coupon elements), aim to count with indicators or use symmetry/conditioning.
  • Set up a recurrence if a direct sum is messy; solve via telescoping or boundary conditions.
  • Always sanity‑check extremes (e.g., as n→∞, as p→0/1).

Problem 4 — Covariance, Variance of Sums, Correlation (12 pts)

  • Start from Var(S)=E[S^2]-(E[S])^2 with S=∑ X_i. Expand E[S^2]=∑ E[X_i^2]+2∑_{i<j} E[X_i X_j].
  • Use Cov(X_i,X_j)=E[X_i X_j]-E[X_i]E[X_j] to write Var(S)=∑ Var(X_i) + 2∑_{i<j} Cov(X_i,X_j).
  • Correlation: ρ=Cov(X,Y)/(σ_X σ_Y) with |ρ|≤1 (Cauchy‑Schwarz). Note: Cov=0 does not imply independence unless special cases (e.g., jointly normal).
  • For indicator vectors, Cov(1_A,1_B)=P(A∩B)-P(A)P(B)—handy for overlaps.

Problem 5 — Conditional Expectation & Total Expectation (14 pts)

  • Compute E[X] by conditioning on a convenient variable/partition: E[X]=∑ E[X|Y=y] P_Y(y) or ∫ E[X|Y=y] f_Y(y) dy.
  • Use the tower property: E[X g(Y)] = E[ E[X|Y] g(Y) ] for measurable g.
  • For variance, remember the law of total variance: Var(X)=E[Var(X|Y)] + Var(E[X|Y]).
  • Choose Y to make conditionals simple (e.g., success counts, mixture components, or sigma‑algebras that render independence).

Problem 6 — Random Walk & Runs (10 pts)

  • Model a simple random walk as S_n=∑_{i=1}^n ξ_i with P(ξ_i=1)=p, P(ξ_i=-1)=q. Then E[S_n]=n(p−q), Var(S_n)=n·4pq.
  • For number of runs in coin tosses, define I_1=1 and I_i=1 if toss i starts a new run; then R=∑ I_i. Compute E[R] via P(I_i=1).
  • For hitting times/first return, set up a recurrence for expectations and solve with boundary conditions (check symmetry at p=1/2).

Problem 7 — MGFs & Distribution Identification (14 pts)

  • Write/recognize the mgf:
    • Bernoulli: M(t)=1-p+pe^t; Poisson: M(t)=exp(λ(e^t−1)); Normal: M(t)=exp(μt + (σ^2 t^2)/2); Gamma(rate λ): M(t)=(λ/(λ−t))^α.
  • Identify a distribution by matching M_X(t) to a known form on a neighborhood of 0 (mgf uniqueness).
  • For sums of independent RVs, multiply mgfs; means/variances follow from derivatives at 0.
  • Confirm domain where M_X(t) exists (some distributions lack mgfs for all t).

Problem 8 — Advanced Expectation Applications (16 pts)

  • Look for a one‑line trick: indicators + linearity, a clever conditioning step, or mgf factorization.
  • Consider Jensen’s inequality for convex/concave functions (e.g., compare E[g(X)] to g(E[X])).
  • If a random sum appears S_N=∑_{i=1}^N X_i with N independent of the i.i.d. X_i, use Wald’s identity: E[S_N]=E[N]E[X_1].
  • When multiple tools could work, prototype both (conditioning vs mgf) on a small special case and keep the cleaner path.

Common pitfalls

  • Using independence where it is not given; remember linearity needs no independence, but Var(∑) simplifications do.
  • Forgetting that Cov=0 does not imply independence in general.
  • Differentiating mgfs without checking their domain (ensure t=0 neighborhood exists).
  • Writing tower laws with the wrong conditioning variable; always verify measurability and supports.
  • Skipping boundary/initial conditions in recurrences for random walks or hitting times.
